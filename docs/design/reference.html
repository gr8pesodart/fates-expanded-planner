Design reference · v2

Paper, ink, and one bright thread.

The planner should feel like a well-kept campaign notebook: warm paper, confident ink, and a single accent colour that follows your route — vermilion for Birthright, amethyst for Conquest, jade for Revelation. Density comes from careful typography and tiny charts, not from boxes and borders. The fun lives in small things: hanko-stamp support ranks, springy presses, growth bars that split personal vs class, and duos that literally stack front-over-back.

Sprites below are monogram placeholders — real class/skill/unit assets drop into the same slots. Numbers are sample data (Ryoma/Camilla/Shiro personal growths are real; class figures illustrative).

Glance first

One row = sprite, name, class chips, sparkline, cap pips. No tables at this level; the tray is where numbers line up.

Pin to compare

Tap-hold (or the pin affordance) adds a unit to the tray. 2–4 pinned. The tray previews the child of any two pinned units that can S-support.

Stamps mean commitment

A hanko appears only once a relationship is set. Conflicts use warn chips, never modals.

Children hang from parents

A dashed thread connects a pair to its child card; averaged growths appear instantly in mono type.

Front on top

A duo is one card: lead above, support below on a slightly sunken band. The role label runs vertically so names stay aligned.

Permanent = ink, planned = faded

Marriages, A+, parents, Corrin choices and inherited skills are full-strength. Class and equipped skills render at 55% opacity.

No controls

No chevrons, no ▾, no buttons. It should print and screenshot cleanly and open straight from a share link.

05

Motion & feel

Springy, short

Presses scale to .95–.98 with --press overshoot; 200–350ms. The lens thumb slides. Nothing fades slower than 250ms.

Numbers tick

When a pairing changes, affected numbers count to their new value (≤300ms) and the delta flashes accent once.

Stamps land

Setting S/A+ drops the hanko in with a tiny scale-down + rotate settle, like it was pressed onto paper.

Sprites are crisp

image-rendering: pixelated for small sprites; never blur-scale. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
